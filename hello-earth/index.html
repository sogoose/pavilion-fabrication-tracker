02 / DESIGN DEVELOPMENT

The pavilion was developed as a reconfigurable architectural system, designed to adapt to different exhibition spaces, layouts, and available quantities of building components.

Rather than creating a fixed structure dependent on one specific arrangement, the design focuses on a single repeatable element that allows the pavilion to take on different forms. This approach shifts the design process from the scale of the pavilion to the scale of the individual brick.

The geometry of the brick emerged from a continuous negotiation between architectural ambition, structural requirements, material efficiency, and robotic fabrication constraints.

Earth performs well under compression, making vertical load transfer an important starting point. However, its high density and the limited material capacity of the robotic printer required a more efficient distribution of material.

Each part of the geometry therefore serves a specific purpose. The cylinders form the primary load-bearing elements, while the triangular arrangement is intended to improve stability against lateral forces. The connecting walls provide continuity between the cylinders while reducing material consumption. The geometry was also developed around the requirements of robotic 3D printing, including the need for a planar printing surface.

All together, these considerations shaped a component that balances structural logic, material use, and fabrication feasibility.

Their triangular organization allows the wall to follow different arrangements, adapting its overall configuration. The result is not one predetermined pavilion, but a building system in which a single component creates multiple architectural possibilities.
